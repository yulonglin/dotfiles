# Sensitive Content Stays In Files

When the material under study is offensive-security or dual-use — exploit code, payloads, exfiltration or C2 endpoints, credentials real or fictional, jailbreak and injection strings, red-team or sabotage-benchmark transcripts — the **analysis** goes in the reply and the **operational specifics** stay in the file. Describe a mechanism in words ("a heap overflow that overwrites a function pointer and uploads model shards to a remote host"); never reproduce payload bodies, working exploit code, hosts, IPs, ports, keys, tokens or injection text in chat, even when they are fictional benchmark artifacts. Give the path and line range instead — Yulong reads raw transcripts himself, and does not need the exploit to make the decision.

This covers every channel a reply lands on (terminal, Slack, email drafts, messages to other sessions) and any tool output relayed into a reply: summarize, do not paste. Subagent briefs quote paths, not payloads. Anything that belongs in the record — a transcript excerpt, a judge rationale that quotes a credential — goes in a file under the results or scratch directory, named in the reply.

**Why:** a server-side safeguards classifier scores the whole request context and the model's own output, not the message you type — so a payload that entered context by any route reads as an attack and triggers a refusal fallback mid-session (observed 2026-09-06 quoting a SleightBench RCE transcript). The route into context is what matters, and chat is only one of them: **`Read`-ing a raw exploit or sabotage-transcript file loads it into context too**, and the next turn ships it to the model whether or not it is ever quoted back. So the payload class is redacted *and* kept out of context — cite the path and a line range, and let Yulong open the file himself. Redaction costs nothing analytically: what matters for detection work is the shape of the sabotage, not its bytes.

**A second trigger class is not fixable by redaction, so do not chase it.** Ordinary research prose dense in threat-model vocabulary — backdoor, exploit, malicious, sabotage, scheming, cyber — trips the same classifier with no payload anywhere (observed 2026-09-06 on a plain literature table, and again while this very session discussed the safeguard itself, tripping on a bare "continue" and a bare "sure"). That vocabulary is the research; rewriting it to dodge the classifier degrades the work. Keep writing normally and, if it matters, `/feedback`.

**What the switch actually is, and how to recover.** The switch is `model_refusal_fallback`, and its target is a hard-coded category map in the CLI, **not** your `fallbackModel` (which governs only the overload path) — a `cyber` flag always lands on Opus 4.8, a `bio` flag on Opus 5, whatever the settings say (the mechanism and evidence are in CLAUDE.md § Learnings). It is `scope:"session"`, so it **latches**: the session stays downgraded until you re-select the model, not just for the flagged turn. Two consequences worth knowing: **`Opus 4.8` on the statusline means you were cyber-downgraded** — nothing else routes there, since the `opus` alias resolves to Opus 5 — and **`/model fable` restores it immediately** rather than waiting the session out. The statusline renders that model in red for exactly this reason.

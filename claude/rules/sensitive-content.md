# Sensitive Content Stays In Files

When the material under study is offensive-security or dual-use — exploit code, payloads, exfiltration or C2 endpoints, credentials real or fictional, jailbreak and injection strings, red-team or sabotage-benchmark transcripts — the **analysis** goes in the reply and the **operational specifics** stay in the file. Describe a mechanism in words ("a heap overflow that overwrites a function pointer and uploads model shards to a remote host"); never reproduce payload bodies, working exploit code, hosts, IPs, ports, keys, tokens or injection text in chat, even when they are fictional benchmark artifacts. Give the path and line range instead — Yulong reads raw transcripts himself, and does not need the exploit to make the decision.

This covers every channel a reply lands on (terminal, Slack, email drafts, messages to other sessions) and any tool output relayed into a reply: summarize, do not paste. Subagent briefs quote paths, not payloads. Anything that belongs in the record — a transcript excerpt, a judge rationale that quotes a credential — goes in a file under the results or scratch directory, named in the reply.

**Why:** the safeguards classifier reads chat text without the surrounding frame, so a quoted benchmark payload reads as an attack and triggers a model fallback mid-session (observed 2026-09-06 quoting a SleightBench RCE transcript). Redaction costs nothing analytically: what matters for detection work is the shape of the sabotage, not its bytes. Tool output and files are not scanned the same way, so that is where the raw material lives.

**A second trigger class is not fixable by redaction, so do not chase it.** Ordinary research prose dense in threat-model vocabulary — backdoor, exploit, malicious, sabotage, scheming, blackmail — also trips the classifier even with no payload present (observed 2026-09-06 on a plain literature table). That vocabulary is the research; rewriting it to dodge the classifier degrades the work. The configured `fallbackModel` does **not** absorb the switch — it governs only the overload and error path. A safeguard flag emits `model_refusal_fallback`, routed by refusal category through a map hard-coded in the CLI, so the model you land on is whatever that release's map names, and it holds across later turns rather than for the flagged turn alone (mechanism and measurements: CLAUDE.md § Learnings, 2026-09-02). Keep writing normally and, if it matters, send feedback — the statusline names the model that is answering. Only the payload class above is worth redacting.
